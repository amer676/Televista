## Key Strategies and Best Practices

Your list is doing more work than your script. Most people get this backwards — they spend hours tweaking their opener while pulling the same tired absentee owner list everyone else in Knox County is calling. Fix the list first.

**Start with stacked data.** A single-source list (just absentee owners, or just pre-foreclosures) leaves money on the table. Cross-referencing equity data, tax delinquency, and ownership duration filters your universe down to people who actually have a reason to sell. Stacking those signals consistently surfaces better conversations — the kind where the seller says "funny you should call, I've been thinking about this."

For building those lists in Tennessee, [PropStream](https://www.propstream.com) and [BatchLeads](https://www.batchleads.io) both pull Knox County records with filtering options that'll let you target by equity percentage, years owned, and pre-foreclosure status simultaneously. Run your skip traces before you dial, not after.

**Timing your calls matters more than most people admit.** Midmorning on weekdays — roughly 9am to 11am Eastern — tends to catch homeowners before the day gets away from them. Early evening, 5pm to 7pm, is worth testing too.> **Pro tip:** Don't rotate through your list linearly. Prioritize by signal strength — leads with two or more motivating factors (tax delinquency *and* high equity, for example) should hit your dialer before single-signal contacts do.

DNC compliance isn't optional. [REDX's guide on the Do-Not-Call list](https://www.redx.com), last updated July 2025, is worth bookmarking — a lawsuit will cost you far more than any deal you'd have closed. Scrub your lists before every campaign, not just when you build them. Numbers rotate on and off the registry.

For your Tennessee script, keep the opener local and specific. Mentioning Knox County by name, or referencing neighborhood conditions you pulled from [Realtor.com's](https://www.realtor.com) housing market trends, signals you're not just blasting a national robo-call. Sellers notice.

Follow-up is where most deals actually close. Six to eight touches across 90 days — calls, texts, maybe a direct mail drop — is a reasonable baseline. Single-touch campaigns are essentially donation runs for your competitors' pipelines.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Most people running a real estate cold calling strategy in Knoxville, TN blow it on compliance before they ever get to the script.

**The DNC list is not optional.** Calling numbers on the National Do Not Call Registry exposes you to lawsuits — and the [REDX guide on DNC compliance](https://www.redx.com) (last updated July 2025) makes clear that real estate investors aren't exempt just because they're not traditional agents. Scrub your list against the registry before every campaign. Not once at setup. Every time.

> **Pro tip:** Run your Knox County list through a DNC scrub automatically, not manually — tools like [REDX's Vortex®](https://www.redx.com) combine lead management with built-in compliance filtering so you're not doing that by hand at midnight.

A few other mistakes worth calling out:

- **Calling without stacked data.** Single-source lists get you hung up on constantly. Cross-referencing equity, tax delinquency, and ownership duration — as we covered in earlier sections — is the difference between a workable list and a waste of a Saturday afternoon.
- **Ignoring timing.** Calling Knoxville homeowners at 8am on a Monday is a skip. Mid-morning Tuesday through Thursday tends to connect better — Knoxville skews suburban, and those households have routines.
- **No follow-up sequence.** One call means nothing. Motivated seller leads in Knoxville rarely convert on contact one. If you're not sequencing through [BatchLeads](https://www.batchleads.io) or a similar CRM, you're leaving callbacks on the table.
- **Generic Tennessee scripts.** Mentioning Knoxville neighborhoods — Bearden, Powell, Halls — in your real estate cold calling script signals you're not a random robocaller. Sellers notice.

Honestly, the biggest mistake is treating cold calling as a one-time push instead of a weekly system. Consistency is what separates investors who close from ones who just have a dialer subscription.
