## Key Strategies and Best Practices

Cold calling real estate leads in Cincinnati isn't complicated — but most investors make it harder than it needs to be by skipping the fundamentals.

**Start with the right list.** Pull absentee owners, pre-foreclosures, and tax-delinquent properties out of [BatchLeads](https://www.batchleads.io) or [PropStream](https://www.propstream.com), then filter for Cincinnati-area zip codes where equity is concentrated. You're not calling everyone. You're calling people who actually have a reason to sell.

Script discipline matters more than most people admit. Not because you should sound robotic — you shouldn't — but because a loose script means you'll fumble the first 15 seconds, and that's the only window you've got. Keep your opener short, name them by first name, and state why you're calling in one sentence. No rambling, no "how are you today." The [MaverickRE blog](https://www.maverickre.com/blog/how-to-practice-cold-calling-real-estate-without-losing-leads) published a solid breakdown on cold calling practice in July 2025 that's worth reading for script refinement — [MaverickRE](https://www.maverickre.com) also offers an AI Call Grading feature that scores your reps' calls automatically, which is a genuinely useful coaching tool if you're building an internal team.

Compliance isn't optional. The [National Association of REALTORS®](https://www.nar.realtor/telemarketing-cold-calling) is direct about it: telemarketing and cold calling are heavily regulated, and scrubbing your lists against the Do Not Call registry before every campaign isn't a nice-to-have. Missing that step can get expensive fast.

> **Pro tip:** Suppress the DNC list *every time* you pull a new list — not just when you first set up your campaign. People register after you've already pulled their number.

Follow-up is where most Cincinnati investors leave deals on the table, honestly. One call isn't a strategy. Build a sequence — first dial, voicemail drop on day two if no answer, callback attempt on day five. Log everything in [REsimpli](https://www.resimpli.com) or a similar CRM so nothing falls through the cracks.

A few other practices worth building into your 2026 cold calling strategy guide for Cincinnati Ohio real estate leads:

- **Call windows:** Tuesday through Thursday, 9–11am and 5–7pm local time tends to get better connect rates
- **Voicemail scripting:** Keep it under 20 seconds — state your name, your interest in their property, and your number once
- **Objection handling:** Write out your top five objections and practice responses out loud, not just in your head

## Step-by-Step Implementation

Pull your list first. Not "kind of pull it" — actually filter it. Go into [BatchLeads](https://www.batchleads.io) or [PropStream](https://www.propstream.com) and export absentee owners, tax-delinquent properties, and pre-foreclosures in your target Cincinnati zip codes. Don't call the whole county. Pick 3-4 neighborhoods, get 200-400 records, and work them properly.

Skip the temptation to just start dialing from a raw export. Skiptrace the list first so you're hitting mobile numbers — landlines waste everyone's time in 2026.

**Here's the actual sequence:**

1. **Build and scrub your list** — [BatchLeads](https://batchleads.io) or [PropStream](https://www.propstream.com), filtered by equity position and property type
2. **Run skiptracing** — BatchLeads does this in-platform; [Mojo Dialer](https://www.mojosells.com) has it built in too
3. **Load into your dialer** — [CallTools](https://www.calltools.com) or Mojo, depending on team size
4. **Check compliance before you dial** — [NAR's telemarketing guidelines](https://www.nar.realtor/telemarketing-cold-calling) are clear that cold calling is heavily regulated, and the DNC list is not optional
5. **Run your calls in blocks** — 2-3 hour sessions, same time each day, mid-morning tends to connect better
6. **Log everything in [REsimpli](https://www.resimpli.com)** — every answer, hang-up, and callback request
7. **Follow up 3-5 times minimum** — most investors quit after one attempt

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Most people running a 2026 cold calling strategy guide for Cincinnati Ohio real estate leads get the fundamentals wrong — not the script, not the dialer, but the stuff that quietly kills campaigns before they get traction.

**Ignoring compliance.** Cold calling and telemarketing are [heavily regulated](https://www.nar.realtor/telemarketing-cold-calling), per NAR — and "I didn't know" isn't a defense when you're dialing numbers on the Do Not Call registry. Scrub your lists. Every time. Don't assume a list you pulled from [BatchLeads](https://www.batchleads.io) is already clean.

Calling too wide, too fast. I've seen investors pull 2,000 records and start hammering through them without any prioritization — then wonder why their appointment rate is terrible. Work 200-400 focused records properly before you ever think about scaling.

> **Pro tip:** If you're skipping follow-up after the first no, you're leaving most of your deals on the table. The second and third touchpoint is where motivated sellers actually open up — the first call is just an introduction.

**No feedback loop on calls.** Tools like [MaverickRE](https://www.maverickre.com) have AI Call Grading and an AI Sales Coach built in — meaning you can actually see where calls are breaking down instead of guessing. Most callers never review their own recordings. That's a fixable problem.

Treating every Cincinnati neighborhood the same. Norwood and Hyde Park aren't the same conversation. Your script shouldn't be either.

One more — and this one's underrated — **not tracking disposition data** inside your CRM, whether that's [REsimpli](https://www.resimpli.com) or something else. If you don't know which list segment is converting, you can't improve anything.

The 2026 cold calling strategy for Cincinnati Ohio real estate leads isn't magic. It's just avoiding the obvious traps most people walk right into.
