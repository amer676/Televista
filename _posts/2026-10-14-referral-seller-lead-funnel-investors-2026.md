You've got the tools, you've got the strategy — now let's actually build the thing.

**Week 1: Audit your existing network.** Pull every closed deal from the last two years out of your CRM (if you're on [RealEstateCRM.io](https://realestatecrm.io/blog/how-to-improve-conversion-rates), this takes maybe 20 minutes). Tag each contact by category: past seller, agent, attorney, property manager, or other professional. Don't overthink the categories — you just need to know who's who before you start reaching out.

**Week 2: Make contact before you ask for anything.** Call or text your top 15-20 past sellers. No pitch. Just a check-in — "Hey, how's the house treating you?" or "Saw something in your neighborhood I thought you'd want to know about." Genuine, low-pressure. You're re-warming relationships that went cold the second closing happened. Most investors skip this step entirely and wonder why their referral asks fall flat.

**Week 3: Build your referral partner list.** Probate attorneys, estate sale companies, divorce mediators, property managers. [Reach out](/contact.html) to 5-10 of these per week with a clear value prop — not "send me deals," but "I close fast, cash, no contingencies, and I'll make you look good to your clients." **Week 4: Set up your tracking and automation.** Every referral source gets a tag in your CRM. Set automated 30/60/90-day follow-up sequences so no one falls through the cracks. ## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Most referral systems don't fail because investors picked the wrong CRM. They fail because someone built the structure, got two leads, and stopped nurturing the relationships that made those leads happen.

**Don't ghost your referral partners after a deal closes.** This is probably the most common one. You close with a probate attorney's referral, you send a thank-you, and then... nothing. Six months later you need leads again and you're starting cold. A quick monthly check-in — even just forwarding a relevant article — keeps you in the rotation without being annoying about it.

Skipping the tracking piece is equally fatal. If you're not tagging referral sources in your CRM, you genuinely don't know what's working. [RealEstateCRM.io](https://realestatecrm.io/blog/how-to-improve-conversion-rates) saves users 10+ hours weekly partly because automations handle this tagging automatically — without it, you're guessing.

> **Pro tip:** If you can't tell me in 30 seconds which referral source sent you the most leads last quarter, your system isn't a system yet. It's just vibes.

A few other traps worth flagging:

- **Over-incentivizing too early.** Cash referral fees are fine, but leading with money before you've built trust often attracts low-quality referrals from people who don't actually know your business
- **No follow-up sequence for referred leads.** Referred sellers still go cold — [REVAS](https://revas.us/real-estate-investor-lead-generation-ideas-from-the-experts/) covers this in their breakdown of motivated seller lead generation
- **Treating all referral partners equally.** An attorney sending you two deals a month deserves a different touchpoint cadence than someone who mentioned you once at a networking event

One more thing — don't obsess over scaling referrals before you've closed your first dozen deals through them. Lock in the process, then scale it.
