## Step-by-Step Implementation

You've done the vetting. You've checked the dialer stack, asked about CRM handoffs, confirmed they can work your list type. Now you actually need to get this thing running — and the first two weeks matter more than most vendors will tell you.

**Step 1: Lock down your list before the caller ever touches the phone.**

Pull your data through [BatchLeads](https://batchleads.io) or [PropStream](https://www.propstream.com), skip-traced and segmented by list type. FSBO leads go in one bucket. Pre-foreclosures in another. Don't hand a cold calling service a scrambled list and expect clean results — that's on you, not them.

**Step 2: Set up your CRM handoff protocol on day one.**

Before the first dial, you need a live pipeline in [REsimpli](https://resimpli.com) or whatever tool you're using, with a defined stage for "caller-qualified lead." Wait until week three to build this and you'll lose leads in the gap. Gaps kill deals.

**Step 3: Run a one-week trial sprint before committing to a full month.**

Some services push back on this — honestly, that tells you something. [Call Savvys](https://callsavvys.com) publishes its pricing at **$1,200/month per caller** with a written guarantee of **20–40 qualified leads per caller per month**, per [Dialing for Dollars](https://dialingfordollars.co/real-estate-cold-calling-companies). If a service has that kind of confidence baked into the contract, they'll usually let you stress-test the first week.

> **Pro tip:** Ask to sit in on live calls or hear recordings during onboarding — not polished demos. [Dialing for Dollars](https://dialingfordollars.co/real-estate-cold-calling-companies) notes that some services play real call recordings during discovery, which is a good sign. If they won't let you hear actual calls, walk away.

**Step 4: Define what "qualified" means before anyone picks up a phone.**

Motivated seller, confirmed ownership, open to a cash offer within 90 days — whatever your threshold is, write it down and make the service sign off on it. Otherwise you'll get 40 leads that are technically alive and totally useless.

**Step 5: Review weekly, not monthly.**

Monthly check-ins are too slow. You'll burn through budget on a broken script and not catch it until it's too late. Weekly call reviews, conversion tracking from dial to appointment, list refresh cadence — that's the rhythm. ## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Most wholesalers don't realize they've picked the wrong vendor until week six. By then, the list is half-burned and the caller's developed bad habits your pipeline will feel for months.

**Don't hire based on price alone.** An hourly shop charging $13–$19/hour (like [Call Force Global](https://www.callforce.com.au)) might look cheap on paper — but with no written lead guarantee, you're just paying for dials. Volume without accountability isn't a deal. It's a drain.

Watch out for vendors that won't play you real call recordings before you sign. Full stop, that's a red flag. [Dialing for Dollars](https://dialingfordollars.co/real-estate-cold-calling-companies) actually highlights this as a differentiator — they play real recordings on a free 30-minute discovery call. If a vendor can't do that, ask yourself why.

> **Pro tip:** Always ask for recordings from the *same list type* you're running — FSBOs, pre-foreclosures, whatever. A caller who's great on absentee owners can still fumble badly on expired listings. I've gone back and forth on this one, but the list-type mismatch is probably the most underrated vetting mistake wholesalers make.

Skipping the CRM handoff question is another one that bites people. Leads need to flow into [REsimpli](https://resimpli.com) or whatever you're running — not sit in a spreadsheet someone emails you on Fridays.

Don't confuse activity metrics with real outcomes, either. Dial counts don't close deals. Qualified appointments do. If your vendor reports dials but not contacts, conversations, or qualified leads, the data's decorative.

One more thing — offshore-based companies aren't automatically bad, but your due diligence has to be tighter. Location alone doesn't predict quality. The written guarantee does.
