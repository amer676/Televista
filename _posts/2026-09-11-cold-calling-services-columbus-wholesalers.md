## Key Strategies and Best Practices

Getting the list right is where most Columbus wholesalers fall flat before they even make a single dial.

[BatchLeads](https://batchleads.io) and [PropStream](https://www.propstream.com) both let you filter by equity percentage, vacancy status, and absentee ownership — which in a market like Columbus means you can target Linden or Hilliard completely differently. High-equity absentee owners in Whitehall behave differently than tax-delinquent homeowners in Grove City. One call script won't cover both. Don't try.

**The core stack for a functioning Columbus outbound operation looks like this:**

- **List source** — [BatchLeads](https://batchleads.io) or [PropStream](https://www.propstream.com), filtered by submarket and motivation indicators (tax delinquency, absentee, pre-foreclosure).
- **Dialer** — [Mojo Dialer](https://www.mojosells.com) for smaller teams, [CallTools](https://calltools.com) for higher volume.
- **CRM** — [REsimpli](https://resimpli.com) is built specifically for wholesalers and handles dispositions without duct-taping five tools together.
- **Follow-up sequence** — SMS + callback within 24 hours, minimum.

Most people overcomplicate this. A simple four-tool stack beats a bloated tech setup every time.

Scripting is the second thing that gets mangled. A cold calling script for real estate wholesalers should open soft — not "I want to buy your house" in the first ten seconds — but also shouldn't meander for two minutes before getting to the point. Something like acknowledging you saw the property's situation (tax status, vacancy) and asking an open question about their plans lands better than a pitch. The goal on the first call isn't a contract. It's a second conversation.

> **Pro tip:** If your caller is getting hung up on constantly in the first 8 seconds, the opening line is the problem — not the list, not the market. Rework the hook before you buy more data.

Consistency beats volume — I've seen this pattern repeatedly. **200 targeted dials per day, five days a week, with proper follow-up** outperforms 500 spray-and-pray calls with no CRM tracking. Columbus is big enough that you've got plenty of runway on a tight list strategy.

One thing that's easy to overlook: DNC compliance. The [FTC's Do Not Call registry](https://www.donotcall.gov) applies whether you're calling yourself or outsourcing to someone else. If you're going the done-for-you route, make sure whoever's running your motivated seller leads in Columbus is scrubbing lists properly — because that liability doesn't disappear when you hand the phones off.

## Step-by-Step Implementation

Pull your list before you do anything else. [BatchLeads](https://batchleads.io) or [PropStream](https://www.propstream.com) — filter by submarket first, not just "Columbus." Absentee owners in Whitehall aren't the same conversation as tax-delinquent landlords in Linden, and your caller needs to know the difference before they pick up the phone.

Here's a workable sequence once your list is ready:

1. **Segment by motivation tier** — high equity + absentee ownership gets dialed first, every time.
2. **Load into your dialer** — [Mojo Dialer](https://www.mojosells.com) or [CallTools](https://calltools.com) both handle Columbus DNC scrubbing automatically, which you don't want to skip.
3. **Run your script with local hooks** — mention the neighborhood, not just "your property." Sellers in Bexley respond differently than sellers in Hilliard.
4. **Log dispositions in your CRM** — [REsimpli](https://resimpli.com) was built for wholesalers specifically; tag callbacks, warm leads, and "not now" separately.
5. **Follow up on warm leads within 24 hours** — the lead doesn't get hotter sitting in a spreadsheet.

Don't overthink the script. A clean open, a curiosity question, and a soft close to an appointment — that's honestly all it needs to be.

> **Pro tip:** If your caller is leaving more than 3 voicemails per list segment without a single callback, the list is probably cold or the skip trace is stale. Pull fresher data before dialing deeper, not after you've burned the whole batch.

Most wholesalers get this backwards — they focus on the script first and treat the list as an afterthought. The list is doing 60% of the work before anyone says hello.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Most Columbus wholesalers don't fail at cold calling because of bad scripts. They fail because of bad habits that compound quietly until their pipeline dries up.

**The biggest one: buying a list and never refreshing it.** Absentee owner data goes stale fast — people sell, die, or resolve their tax issues — and if you're dialing off a six-month-old pull from [PropStream](https://www.propstream.com) without re-filtering, you're burning caller time on contacts that don't match your criteria anymore. Run fresh pulls by submarket every 4-6 weeks minimum.

Skipping the CRM step is another one that kills momentum. A caller logs a "callback requested" in a spreadsheet, it gets missed, and that motivated seller in Whitehall talks to your competitor instead. [REsimpli](https://resimpli.com) or even a basic [HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com) setup prevents this — the follow-up sequence has to be automatic, not manual.

> **Pro tip:** Don't assign your best caller to your worst list. Seems obvious, but it happens constantly. Segment by motivation tier first, then decide who's working which leads.

Chasing every submarket at once is overrated, honestly. Columbus is big enough that spreading dials across Gahanna, Linden, Grove City, and Hilliard simultaneously with no submarket-specific framing just produces confused callers and generic conversations. Pick two or three pockets and go deep.

And don't underestimate [DNC compliance](https://www.ftc.gov/consumers/consumer-information/do-not-call-registry) — scrubbing your list before every campaign isn't optional. One complaint can trigger a review that shuts your whole operation down.

If you're outsourcing and don't have a clear handoff protocol between your callers and your acquisitions team, the leads will leak. Every time.
