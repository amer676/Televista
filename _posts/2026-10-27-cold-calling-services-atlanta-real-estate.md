## Atlanta-Specific Lead Types That Change How You Should Call

Metro Atlanta isn't one market. It's a dozen micro-markets stacked on top of each other — and the lead type you're working determines almost everything about how your callers should open, pace, and close a conversation.

**Probate leads** are one of the highest-converting list types in Fulton and DeKalb counties, where filings are consistent and public records are relatively accessible via [PropStream](https://www.propstream.com). But probate calls require a completely different emotional register. You're often talking to someone who just lost a parent — a caller who opens with the same energy they'd use on a tax-delinquent list is going to burn that relationship in the first 15 seconds. Soft tone, slower pace, genuine patience. Not just a tactic; it's the minimum.

Tax-delinquent properties are a different animal entirely. Georgia's tax lien process has specific timelines (the redemption window matters a lot), and callers who don't understand where a property sits in that timeline will push way too hard or not hard enough. Neither works.

Absentee owners in transitional neighborhoods — Westside, South Atlanta, parts of East Point — are often sitting on equity they don't fully realize is there. That's a financial conversation, not an emotional one.

[All The Leads](https://alltheleads.com) tracks probate, pre-foreclosure, absentee, tax-delinquent, and vacant property lists because serious investors need that granularity. A service that treats every list like a generic homeowner campaign doesn't understand this business.

> **Pro tip:** Ask any cold calling service you're vetting how their callers handle a probate list differently from absentee owners. Blank stare? That's your answer.

Atlanta REIA events draw active, organized investors every month — the community here knows its markets. If you're outsourcing calls, your callers should at least understand what neighborhoods you're targeting and why. That context shows up in conversations. Prospects feel it.

## 7 Steps to Vet a Cold Calling Service Before You Sign Anything

Before you hand over a list and a check, run every service through these seven checks — no exceptions.

**1. Ask to hear a live call or a recording on a probate or absentee owner list.** Won't let you hear a recording? Walk away. Any service worth hiring has nothing to hide.

**2. Confirm their callers understand what a motivated seller actually sounds like.** Script readers will get you contacts. Trained callers will get you appointments. Big difference, and you can usually hear it in 90 seconds.

**3. Ask about their skiptracing source and how often it refreshes.** Dead numbers don't just waste time — they tank your contact rate and burn out your callers. [DataSift's](https://www.datasift.com) unlimited skiptracing model is a useful benchmark for what ongoing data hygiene should look like.

**4. Test the CRM handoff before anything else.** Can they push leads directly into [REsimpli](https://resimpli.com), [HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com), or whatever you're running? Manual CSV exports in 2026 are a dealbreaker — don't accept them.

**5. Ask for their contact rate on cold lists, not just their close rate.** Close rate is partly their callers' skill and partly your follow-up. Contact rate is a clean read on their data quality and dialer setup.

**6. Check whether their scripts are built for emotionally sensitive calls.** Probate, divorce, pre-foreclosure — these aren't the same as a standard homeowner call. [ALL THE LEADS](https://alltheleads.com) publishes checklists and net sheets that show what real scripting infrastructure looks like for these call types. That's the bar.

**7. Run a short pilot first.** Four to six weeks, a defined list segment, clear KPIs. Any service that pushes back on a pilot — or buries a minimum commitment in the contract — is telling you something about how confident they are in their own results.

> **Pro tip:** Don't just check what a service does. Check what happens when something goes wrong. Ask them directly: "What's your process if contact rates drop in week two?" Their answer will tell you more than any sales deck.
