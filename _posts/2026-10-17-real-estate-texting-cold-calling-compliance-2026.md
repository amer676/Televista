Real estate outreach — cold calling, SMS campaigns, ringless voicemails — sits at the intersection of aggressive lead generation and a tightening regulatory web. And that tension isn't going away.

[NAR acknowledges it directly](https://www.nar.realtor/telemarketing-cold-calling): telemarketing and cold calling are heavily regulated, yet they remain two of the most reliable ways to build and maintain your contact list. That's the paradox every investor and agent is living in right now. You need the outreach. You can't ignore the rules.

So what does "compliance" actually mean in this context? It's not just a checklist. It's the overlap between three distinct layers of regulation:

- **TCPA (Telephone Consumer Protection Act)** — governs autodialed calls, prerecorded messages, and text messages to cell phones
- **The National Do Not Call Registry** — managed by the FTC, with real enforcement teeth
- **[NAR's Code of Ethics](https://www.nar.realtor/telemarketing-cold-calling)** — professional conduct standards that apply on top of federal law

Most people treat these as separate problems. They're not — a single outreach campaign can trip all three simultaneously.

The regulatory pressure is only intensifying. On November 22, 2023, the [FCC released a Second Report and Order](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/fcc-proposes-new-stricter-tcpa-rules-1484625/) proposing stricter TCPA rules that would limit lead generation to **one-company consent**. Meaning a lead opting into one real estate platform wouldn't automatically give *your* company permission to call them. That's a huge shift from how shared lead lists have worked for years.

> **Key Stat:** A $40 million class-action settlement — preliminarily approved by a Florida court — shows exactly what TCPA non-compliance looks like at scale for a real estate company, per [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=fdec82a1-b51b-47ff-925a-fe0e93248b3c).

Figuring out 2026 outreach compliance means understanding where these rules apply, how they've changed, and — honestly — which mistakes are so common that even experienced operators make them without realizing it. That's what the rest of this piece covers.

> **Pro tip:** Don't wait for a complaint to audit your consent practices. By the time someone files, you're already behind.

[REsimpli](https://resimpli.com) handles this reasonably well on the CRM side — your opt-outs log automatically, which matters if you're ever audited. ## Step-by-Step Implementation

Getting compliant isn't a one-time project. It's a workflow — and if you bolt it on after the fact, you're already behind.

**Step 1: Audit your consent records before you dial or text anything.**

Pull every list you're currently using and ask one question: can you prove, in writing, that each contact consented to hear from *your company specifically*? Not a partner. Not a lead gen aggregator. You. The [FCC's November 2023 Second Report and Order](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/fcc-proposes-new-stricter-tcpa-rules-1484625/) is pushing toward one-company consent, which means shared opt-in language from a third-party form won't cut it. Trash the lists you can't verify. I know that stings, but the alternative is a $40 million problem.

**Step 2: Scrub against the DNC Registry — every single time.**

Don't do this quarterly. Do it before every campaign. [Mojo Dialer](https://www.mojosells.com) and [CallTools](https://www.calltools.com) both offer built-in DNC scrubbing; use it. [BatchLeads](https://batchleads.io) can layer on litigator lists too, which most people skip (those lists flag serial TCPA plaintiffs — worth the extra step).

**Step 3: Build a consent capture process for every new lead source.**

Going forward, every form, every landing page, every opt-in needs to name your company explicitly. No vague "partners may contact you" language. Log timestamps and IP addresses. [REsimpli](https://resimpli.com) has decent lead capture fields where you can attach consent notes directly to contact records — keeps everything in one place.

**Step 4: Train anyone who touches the phone or sends a text.**

Your callers need to know calling hours (8am–9pm local time, per the TCPA), how to handle a "do not call" request immediately, and what they can and can't say. [NAR's telemarketing guidance](https://www.nar.realtor/telemarketing-cold-calling) is a free starting point for your training doc.

> **Pro tip:** Build a simple compliance checklist your callers run through before each session — list scrubbed today? Calling hours confirmed? Opt-out script memorized? Takes two minutes, saves you five figures minimum.

**Step 5: Document everything, permanently.**

Consent records, call logs, opt-out requests — keep them. TCPA claims can surface years after a campaign ends.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Most people don't get hit with a TCPA lawsuit because they were malicious. They get hit because they were sloppy — and sloppy is a lot easier to fix than most operators realize.

**Mistake #1: Treating old consent as permanent.**

Consent expires. Someone who filled out a form two years ago to hear from a different company didn't consent to hear from you — and [the FCC's proposed rules](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/fcc-proposes-new-stricter-tcpa-rules-1484625/) are tightening exactly this gap, moving toward one-company consent that would invalidate shared lead pools entirely. If your list came from an aggregator, you don't have clean consent. Full stop.

**Mistake #2: Relying on auto-dial features without checking ATDS classification.**

[Mojo Dialer](https://www.mojosells.com) and [CallTools](https://www.calltools.com) both have power dialing modes that could trigger ATDS classification depending on configuration. I've seen teams flip on every speed feature available without reading the fine print. Don't do that.

**Mistake #3: No DNC scrub before texting.**

Texting feels casual. It isn't — not legally. The same rules that apply to calls apply to SMS. [NAR's own guidance](https://www.nar.realtor/telemarketing-cold-calling) acknowledges how heavily regulated these channels are, and a lot of agents still treat texts like they're DMing a friend.

> **Pro tip:** Build DNC scrubbing into your list prep, not as an afterthought before you hit send. If it's not baked into your [BatchLeads](https://www.batchleads.io) or [PropStream](https://www.propstream.com) export workflow, you're one bad list away from trouble.

**Mistake #4: Assuming you're too small to get sued.**

A privately held real estate company — not a Wall Street firm — faced a [$40 million class-action settlement](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=fdec82a1-b51b-47ff-925a-fe0e93248b3c) over TCPA violations. Size didn't protect them. Volume did the damage.
