## Is Wholesaling Legal in Florida? What You Need to Know Before You Dial

Yes. Wholesaling is legal in Florida. Get that out of your head right now — it's a question that trips up newer investors who've heard horror stories from other states.

But there are guardrails, and ignoring them is how you get in trouble.

Florida's [Chapter 686 of the Florida Statutes](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter686) governs contracts broadly and provides the foundation for how assignment agreements — the backbone of a wholesale deal — hold up legally. Your purchase and sale agreement needs to explicitly allow assignment, or you've got a problem before the conversation even starts.

The bigger shift happened with **Florida SB 730**, which passed in 2023. Wholesalers are now required to disclose their equitable interest in any marketing materials related to a property they're under contract on. You can't market a property you don't own or have rights to without making that clear. — I'd consult a Florida real estate attorney before you send a single marketing email or text blast, honestly. The disclosure language matters more than most people think.

Now, what does this mean for cold calling specifically?

Callers soliciting motivated seller leads aren't acting as licensed agents — they're not doing brokerage activity by making contact. The *deal structure* after that call is where the legal weight sits. That distinction matters when you're running an [outsourced cold calling](https://televistaleadgeneration.com/blog/2026/09/23/outsourced-cold-calling-knoxville-real-estate-2026/) operation.

**DNC compliance is non-negotiable.** Florida maintains its own state do-not-call list under [Chapter 501, Florida Statutes](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter501), separate from the [National DNC Registry](https://www.donotcall.gov). Your lists have to be scrubbed against both before anyone dials. Any serious cold calling service — including [Televista](/services.html) — should be handling this as a baseline, not an afterthought.

> **Pro tip:** Don't treat DNC scrubbing as a one-time thing. Lists go stale fast. Scrub before every new campaign push, not just once when you build the list.

Get your legal framework right first. Then dial.

### Which Two Contracts Do You Need for Wholesaling in Florida?

Two. That's it. Most new wholesalers overcomplicate this.

**1. Purchase and Sale Agreement**  
Signed between you and the seller. This locks up the property at an agreed price and gives you equitable interest — which is what you're actually selling when you wholesale. Florida's [Chapter 475 of the Florida Statutes](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter475) distinguishes between selling a property and selling a contract interest, and that distinction matters.

**2. Assignment of Contract (or Double-Close Agreement)**  
This transfers your interest in that Purchase and Sale Agreement to your end buyer — for a fee. [Florida courts have consistently upheld assignments of contract](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/Chapter686) in real estate transactions, so the model's legally sound when your paperwork is structured right. If your buyer won't accept an assignment (happens sometimes with institutional buyers), a double-close is your fallback.

> **Pro tip:** Get a Florida real estate attorney to review your assignment language before you run your first deal — not after. A $300 consult now beats a blown deal later.

## What Makes a Cold Calling Script Work for [Real Estate Wholesalers](https://televistaleadgeneration.com/blog/2026/08/01/cold-calling-pittsburgh-real-estate-wholesalers/)

No single "magic script" exists. Anyone selling you one is selling you false hope.

The best cold call script for a WPB wholesaler depends on your list, your caller's natural persona, and — most importantly — the seller's specific situation. An absentee owner in Lake Worth responds differently than someone who inherited a property in Riviera Beach and hasn't touched it in three years. Script accordingly.

**The anatomy of a motivated seller call that actually converts:**

1. **Pattern interrupt opening** — Don't start with "Hi, how are you today?" Sellers hang up on that in two seconds flat. Open with something specific to their situation: "I noticed this property hasn't been owner-occupied for a while — I'm reaching out to see if you'd consider an offer."

2. **Clear reason tied to their situation** — absentee status, inherited title, tax delinquency. Generic openers tank connect rates. **Personalization by list type is non-negotiable.**

3. **One open-ended question** that surfaces motivation — "What's your situation with the property right now?" works better than anything clever.

4. **Soft close** — appointment or callback, not a hard pitch.

> **Pro tip:** Bake objection handlers directly into the script before you go live. "I'm not interested" and "I'm not selling" are what your callers will hear the majority of the time — they need pre-loaded responses, not improvised fumbling.

Tools like [REsimpli](https://resimpli.com) and [BatchLeads](https://batchleads.io) can tag seller motivation data right in the CRM, which feeds directly into better script personalization before a caller ever dials.
