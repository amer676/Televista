## Key Strategies and Best Practices

The script matters less than most people think. What actually separates brokers who book meetings consistently from ones who don't is the work that happens *before* anyone picks up a phone.

**Start with a tighter list.** Calling every business in your metro is a waste of time and rep morale. Pull from tools like [BatchLeads](https://www.batchleads.io) or [PropStream](https://www.propstream.com) if you're doing real estate-adjacent commercial work — but for straight commercial insurance prospecting, build your list around SIC codes, employee count ranges, and renewal timing if you can get it.**Call the right person, not just the right company.** Business owners — not office managers, not HR — are your buyers for commercial coverage. Your callers need to be trained to deal with gatekeepers without burning the relationship. No combative tactics, no lying about what the call is about. Just confident, direct positioning: "I'm reaching out to schedule 15 minutes with [owner name] to see if there's a fit worth exploring on the commercial side."

Script structure matters, but keep it loose. The best callers for outbound cold calling for insurance brokers sound like they're having a conversation, not reading a card. Train to objections — "we're happy with our current broker" is the one you'll hear most — and have a one-line pivot ready that doesn't feel canned.

> **Pro tip:** Don't pitch coverage on a cold call. Ever. Your only goal is to make the meeting sound low-risk. "15 minutes, no obligation, just want to see if what we do is even relevant for your situation" books more appointments than any feature dump.

Compliance isn't optional. The [FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-telemarketing-sales-rule) — last updated October 2026 — covers for-profit organizations running outbound calls, and insurance agencies aren't exempt. Scrubbing against the Do Not Call registry, maintaining call records, and training callers on disclosure requirements should be non-negotiables in any commercial insurance lead generation calls program you run.

Cadence is where most in-house efforts fall apart. One call isn't a campaign. Voicemail on attempt one, live connect attempt two, follow-up email same day — that's a sequence, not a one-shot. ## Step-by-Step Implementation

You've got your list, your dialer, your script. Actually running this thing — that's where most brokers stall out.

Here's the sequence that works, in order.

**Step 1: Build the right list first.** Don't skip this. Pull SIC codes that match your target verticals — contractors, manufacturers, trucking, retail — and filter by employee count and geography. Tools like [SalesQL](https://www.salesql.com) or [Apollo.io](https://www.apollo.io) can help you get verified business owner contacts without paying for a ton of junk data.

**Step 2: Scrub against the DNC registry before anything goes into your dialer.** Not optional. The [FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-telemarketing-sales-rule) — last updated October 8, 2026 — covers commercial outbound calling, and enforcement isn't something you want to test. Run every list through a scrubbing service. Then load clean records into [CallTools](https://www.calltools.com) or [Mojo Dialer](https://www.mojosells.com) depending on team size.

**Step 3: Set your daily dial targets and cadence.** Aim for consistent volume — a rep running 150–200 dials a day on a power dialer, say. Build in 3-touch sequences: call, voicemail drop, follow-up call 48 hours later. Most business owners don't respond to the first attempt. They rarely do.

> **Pro tip:** Keep the opening under 10 seconds. Business owners — especially in construction and manufacturing — hang up fast if they sniff a pitch. Lead with their industry and a reason, not your agency name.

**Step 4: Log everything in your CRM immediately after each call.** Disposition codes matter more than people realize. "Not interested now" and "call back in 60 days" are completely different buckets, and mixing them up kills follow-up quality. [REsimpli](https://www.resimpli.com) handles this cleanly if you're doing real estate-adjacent commercial work; for pure insurance pipelines, [HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com) works fine.

**Step 5: Book the meeting into your producer's calendar in real time** — not via email later. If your caller has to send a "someone will follow up" message, you've already lost half the appointments.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Most brokers who try outbound calling and quit aren't failing because cold calling doesn't work. They're failing because of a handful of fixable mistakes — and they never figure out which one killed their pipeline.

**Mistake 1: Calling without scrubbing against the Do Not Call registry.** Skipping this isn't just sloppy — it's a legal exposure. The [FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-telemarketing-sales-rule), last modified October 2026, is explicit about who must comply. Most commercial outreach gets some B2B exemption carve-out, but that protection isn't blanket and the rules have teeth. Build DNC scrubbing into your list prep, not as an afterthought.

**Mistake 2: Treating appointment setting like a part-time task.** Consistency is the whole game. A rep making 40 calls on Monday and 12 on Thursday produces garbage data and worse results. Dialers like [CallTools](https://www.calltools.com) and [Mojo Dialer](https://www.mojosells.com) help enforce session discipline — skip activity tracking and you're flying blind.

> **Pro tip:** Set a minimum daily dial count and don't let reps skip it "just for today." That exception becomes the norm inside two weeks. Trust me on this one.

**Mistake 3: Using a generic script across every vertical.** A trucking company owner doesn't have the same pain points as a restaurant owner. Same pitch, same indifference.

**Mistake 4: No follow-up sequence.** Most commercial insurance appointments come from the second or third contact — not the first call. If your team dials once and marks a lead dead, they're leaving meetings on the table. Build a touchpoint sequence into your CRM from day one.

TCPA compliance for outbound calling for insurance brokers isn't glamorous, but it protects your license and your agency's reputation.
