## Step-by-Step Implementation

You've got your data, your dialer, your script. Building the system in the right order — that's where most El Paso investors trip up.

**Step 1: Pull your list first, not last.**

Open [PropStream](https://www.propstream.com) and filter for absentee owners, tax-delinquent properties, and high-equity homes in your target zip codes. Don't skip the skip trace step. A raw list without good phone numbers is just a spreadsheet you'll never use.

**Step 2: Scrub against the DNC registry** before a single dial goes out. Texas takes this seriously — the [Texas Real Estate Commission](https://www.trec.texas.gov) publishes consumer protection standards that apply to how you contact property owners, and ignoring that isn't a gray area. Get familiar with TREC's Consumer Protection Notice requirements (the kind licensed agents post publicly, like [this reference here](https://www.facebook.com/ChristyHarrisRealEstateAgent/posts/texas-real-estate-commission-consumer-protection-notice-texas-real-estate-commis/101990817948934/)) — even as an investor, you want to understand what regulated disclosure obligations look like in your market.

**Step 3: Load into your dialer.** [Mojo Dialer](https://www.mojosells.com) handles multi-line calling well for solo operators. Bigger team? [CallTools](https://www.calltools.com) gives you more admin control. Either way, segment your list by neighborhood before you start — East El Paso doesn't call the same as the west side, and you'll hear it in the conversations.

> **Pro tip:** Don't batch your entire list into one campaign. Break it into 200-300 number chunks, run for a week, review your connect rate and what objections you're hearing, then adjust your script before the next batch. Most people skip this review step entirely and wonder why nothing converts.

**Step 4: Track everything in [REsimpli](https://www.resimpli.com).** Log dispositions immediately — not at the end of the day. Memory is unreliable and you'll conflate calls. "Callback requested" and "not interested" need to live in different buckets from the jump.

**Step 5: Follow up.** Relentlessly. The first call rarely closes anything — motivated sellers in El Paso often need 3-5 touches before they're ready to talk seriously. Build your follow-up sequences before you launch, not after you've already called everyone once.

|---|---|---|
| List Building | Filter + skip trace | PropStream / BatchLeads |
| Compliance Scrub | DNC check | TREC guidelines + state registry |
| Dialing | Multi-line outbound | Mojo / CallTools |
| CRM Tracking | Disposition logging | REsimpli |
| Follow-Up | Sequenced callbacks | REsimpli / Mojo |

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Most investors running cold calling for real estate investors in El Paso Texas campaigns fail in the same three places. Not unique places. The same ones, over and over.

**Skipping compliance checks.** Texas isn't a state where you can wing the legal stuff. The [Texas Real Estate Commission](https://www.facebook.com/ChristyHarrisRealEstateAgent/posts/texas-real-estate-commission-consumer-protection-notice-texas-real-estate-commis/101990817948934/) puts out a Consumer Protection Notice and information about brokerage services that agents and investors operating in Texas need to actually understand — not skim. Calling without knowing where your obligations start and stop is how you create expensive problems fast.

**Using bad data and not knowing it.** Pulling a list from [BatchLeads](https://www.batchleads.io) or [PropStream](https://www.propstream.com) and never refreshing it is a quiet killer. El Paso's absentee owner lists shift constantly — military families at Fort Bliss move on 2-3 year cycles, which means your list from eight months ago has already rotted in chunks. (I've gone back and forth on how often to re-scrub, but 60-90 days is where I've landed.)

> **Pro tip:** Schedule your re-scrubs before you launch, not after you've already burned a week on disconnected numbers. I've seen callers waste entire weeks simply because nobody put a re-scrub date on the calendar.

Calling without a real script — or clinging too hard to one — is another one. There's a middle ground. Go in with structure, not a monologue.

A few other mistakes worth flagging:

- **Dialing at the wrong times.** Late afternoon on weekdays typically converts better than midday
- **No CRM discipline.** If you're not logging outcomes in [REsimpli](https://www.resimpli.com) or a comparable tool, you're flying blind on follow-up
- **Giving up after one contact attempt.** Most motivated sellers don't say yes on call one

Long story short — cleaner data, a solid script framework, and consistent follow-up solves about 80% of what kills El Paso cold calling campaigns before they get traction.
