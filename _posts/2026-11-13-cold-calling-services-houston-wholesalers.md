## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Most wholesalers don't lose deals in the negotiation. They lose them in the setup — bad lists, wrong callers, zero compliance awareness.

**Skipping the DNC scrub.** Texas's telephone solicitation law now covers text messages as of [September 1, 2025](https://nationalpawnbrokers.org/2025/10/06/urgent-texas-text-messages-covered-under-telephone-solicitation-law-effective-september-1/). If your freelancer or in-house caller is blasting texts to unscrubbed lists, you're exposed — and TCPA exposure isn't theoretical. The [Illinois Supreme Court ruled TCPA damages are remedial, not punitive](https://kleinmoynihan.com/tcpa-damages-are-remedial-not-punitive/), which sounds friendlier until you do the math on per-violation liability at scale.

Don't assume your VA knows any of this.

**Confusing activity with output.** A caller logging 300 dials a day sounds productive. But if they're not tracking connect rates, conversations, and booked appointments separately — in [REsimpli](https://www.resimpli.com) or a basic [HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com) pipeline — you have no idea what's actually working. I've seen teams run hot for 60 days and have nothing to show for it because nobody was tracking the right metrics. Long story short — dials are vanity, appointments are what pay.

> **Pro tip:** Set up your CRM to track connects, conversations, and appointments from day one. Not after the campaign already ran.

**Hiring for price, not fit.** The cheapest freelancer on Upwork won't know Houston neighborhoods, won't handle objections on inherited properties, and probably won't last past week three. Real estate wholesaling cold calling requires domain knowledge. Generic VA work doesn't translate.

One more — and people overlook this constantly — **not having a callback system**. Distressed Houston sellers often don't pick up the first time. Drop a voicemail and move on, and you're leaving off-market deals on the table every single day.
