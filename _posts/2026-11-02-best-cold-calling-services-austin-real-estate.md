## Key Strategies and Best Practices

Austin's cold calling game has one rule above everything else: **speed to lead wins**. Not better scripts. Not fancier dialers. Speed.

But speed without structure just burns your list faster.

Here's what actually separates investors who consistently pull motivated seller leads in Austin from the ones grinding the same tired lists with nothing to show for it.

---

**Start with the right list — not the biggest one.**

Most people get this backwards. They pull 10,000 records from [BatchLeads](https://batchleads.io) or [PropStream](https://propstream.com) and just start dialing — then wonder why their callers are exhausted and their appointment rate is garbage. Tighter, better-segmented lists — absentee owners, tax delinquents, pre-foreclosures — almost always outperform a massive unfocused pull. Less volume, more intent.

> **Pro tip:** Before you dial a single number, scrub your list against the DNC registry and verify it through a skip tracing service. Sounds obvious. Most people skip it anyway.

**Dial volume matters, but it has a ceiling.**

[David Cummings](https://davidcummings.org/2015/09/23/how-much-does-a-single-cold-call-cost/) puts a full-time cold caller at roughly **100 calls per day** — about 12.5 per hour across a 2,000-hour work year. That's your baseline to measure against. If your caller (or your outsourced team) is hitting noticeably less than that, something's broken — the dialer, the list, or the process. Speaking of dialers: power dialers like [Mojo Dialer](https://mojosells.com) can multiply that output, and triple-line dialing gets you more connections per hour, but don't let anyone tell you it replaces a well-trained voice on the other end.

**Disposition every call. Every single one.**

Dead leads today aren't dead forever — especially in a market as dynamic as Austin. If someone says "call me back in 6 months," that follow-up is where deals come from. Use [REsimpli](https://resimpli.com) or a similar CRM to tag, track, and sequence callbacks automatically. Most investors lose money not because of bad calls, but because they don't follow up.

A few other things worth building into your process:

- **Test two scripts at minimum** — one pain-focused ("are you open to selling?"), one curiosity-focused ("I noticed your property, I buy in that area")
- **Call back within 24 hours** of any voicemail response
- **Rotate list segments weekly** to avoid caller fatigue on the same names

[Televista](/services.html) handles all of this inside a single managed workflow — list sourcing, scripting, disposition tagging — so you're not duct-taping five tools together yourself. But whether you're building it in-house or outsourcing, the structure matters more than any individual tactic.

> **Key Stat:** At a fully burdened cost of [$43,200 per year](https://davidcummings.org/2015/09/23/how-much-does-a-single-cold-call-cost/) for one full-time caller (salary + taxes + benefits), every inefficiency in your process has a real dollar cost attached to it.

Outsourced cold calling for real estate investors isn't a shortcut. It's a systems decision. And in Austin's market, bad systems lose deals fast.

## Step-by-Step Implementation

Most investors know they *should* be cold calling. Actually building the system is where things fall apart.

Here's the honest sequence — no fluff, just the order of operations that doesn't waste your first two weeks.

---

**Step 1: Pull a targeted list, not a massive one.**

Start with [BatchLeads](https://batchleads.io) or [PropStream](https://propstream.com) and filter for absentee owners, tax delinquents, or pre-foreclosures in the Austin ZIP codes you actually want to work. Don't dump 50,000 records into a dialer on day one. You'll burn through your list and have nothing left worth calling.

**Step 2: Set up your dialer before you touch the list.**

[Mojo Dialer](https://www.mojodialer.com) or [CallTools](https://calltools.com) — pick one, configure your disposition drop-downs, and connect it to your CRM. [REsimpli](https://resimpli.com) works well here if you want calling and deal tracking in one place. Skip this step and your callers are dropping notes in a spreadsheet while you fly blind.

**Step 3: Decide — in-house or outsourced?**

This is the fork in the road. According to [David Cummings](https://davidcummings.org/2015/09/23/how-much-does-a-single-cold-call-cost/), a full-time cold caller running **100 dials per day** costs roughly **$43,200 per year** fully burdened (salary, taxes, benefits) — and that's before training, turnover, or management time. For a lot of solo investors in Austin, outsourcing to a team like [Televista](/services.html) makes more sense on pure math: callers are already trained, already dialing, and you're not managing HR headaches.

> **Pro tip:** Don't hire a cold caller and hand them a raw list on Monday. Spend a week on the script, objection handling, and disposition criteria *first* — or your first month of data is garbage.

**Step 4: Run a pilot week before scaling.**

Dial one segment, one ZIP, one list type. See where calls are dying. Adjust the script before you add volume.

**Step 5: Review dispositions weekly — not monthly.**

Pull your "call back," "not interested," and "follow up" buckets every Friday. The gold in Austin motivated seller leads is usually sitting in the follow-up pile, untouched. Long story short — slow rollout beats fast burnout. Every time.
