## Step-by-Step Implementation

Getting a cold calling campaign off the ground in Fort Lauderdale isn't complicated — but the order of operations matters more than most investors think. Skip a step and you're burning dials on dead lists or getting hung up before the conversation even starts.

**Step 1: Build a targeted list first.** Pull motivated seller leads from [BatchLeads](https://www.batchleads.io) or [PropStream](https://www.propstream.com) — filter by absentee ownership, tax delinquency, or pre-foreclosure in Broward County specifically. Fort Lauderdale zip codes vary wildly in seller motivation, so don't just upload a county-wide list and call it done.

**Step 2: Run your list through skip-tracing.** Bad numbers kill connect rates. Use [BatchLeads](https://batchleads.io)' built-in skip-trace or a standalone service before loading anything into your dialer.

**Step 3: Load into your dialer and set up CRM tagging.** [Mojo Dialer](https://mojosells.com) works well for residential list-heavy campaigns. [REsimpli](https://resimpli.com) handles CRM + dialer in one place if you want fewer moving parts.

**Step 4: Script for Fort Lauderdale specifically.** Generic "are you looking to sell?" openers get hung up on constantly. Reference the neighborhood, mention the current market pressure — make it feel local. (I've seen investors use the same script they used in Ohio and wonder why Florida sellers aren't biting. Different market, different tone.)

**Step 5: Dial, log, and follow up religiously.** First call rarely closes anything. Tag every lead by status in your CRM and run a consistent follow-up cadence — most motivated sellers need 3–5 touches before they're ready to talk numbers.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Most Fort Lauderdale investors don't fail at cold calling because the channel doesn't work. They fail because they're making the same avoidable errors — over and over.

**Calling without scrubbing your list first.** Florida has active Do Not Call enforcement, and real estate fraud litigation is a real exposure risk — [Ayala Law PA](https://www.lawayala.com/real-estate-fraud-litigation/) lays out exactly how deceptive practices in real estate transactions can land you in litigation. Skipping DNC compliance isn't a shortcut. It's a liability.

Chasing vanity metrics instead of conversations. A hundred dials a day means nothing if your callers are hanging up the moment someone pushes back. Track connect rate and appointments set — not just dials.

> **Pro tip:** If your caller can't handle a "not interested" without bailing, you don't have a dialer problem. You have a training problem. Script depth and objection handling matter more than your tech stack.

**Ignoring caller ID health.** Numbers get flagged fast — especially in high-volume markets. Rotate numbers regularly inside whatever dialer you're using, whether that's [Mojo Dialer](https://mojosells.com) or [CallTools](https://calltools.com). Dead caller ID kills connect rates quietly.

Lots of investors also jump straight into outsourcing without having any internal feedback loop. You can't manage what you can't measure — if you're not reviewing call recordings or tracking outcomes in [REsimpli](https://resimpli.com) or a similar CRM, you'll never know if the campaigns are actually working.

One more thing that trips people up: picking a service built for B2B outreach and expecting it to nail motivated seller conversations. [RhemaVox](https://rhemavox.com/industries/real-estate/) is specifically structured for commercial and investment-side outreach — which is great for the right use case, but it's not the same as residential wholesale. **Match the service to the actual call type you need.**
