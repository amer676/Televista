## Key Strategies and Best Practices

Broward County's eviction pressure is real — [18,670 filings in 2023 alone](https://flhousingdata.shimberg.ufl.edu/eviction-foreclosure/results?nid=600), and projections put 2025 at roughly **16,666** — but raw data doesn't close deals. How you work that list does.

Most wholesalers get this backwards. They spend hours pulling records from [BatchLeads](https://www.batchleads.io) or [PropStream](https://www.propstream.com) and then call with a generic script. The list isn't the problem. The conversation is.

**Start with list segmentation before you dial anything.** Pre-foreclosure, eviction-adjacent, absentee owner — each of those seller profiles has a different emotional state and a different objection. A landlord facing eviction court costs in Broward isn't in the same headspace as an absentee owner who inherited a property in Lauderdale Lakes. Your script needs to reflect that.

> **Pro tip:** Build 2-3 script variants instead of one universal opener. Your eviction-list call should acknowledge financial pressure quickly — something like "I know things have been tight for a lot of landlords lately." Your absentee owner script can be softer, more curiosity-based. The goal isn't to sound slick; it's to sound relevant.

On the dialing side, local presence matters more than most people admit. Calling into South Florida from an out-of-state number tanks your answer rate — full stop. Tools like [CallTools](https://www.calltools.com) and [Mojo Dialer](https://www.mojosells.com) both support local caller ID rotation, and in a market like Fort Lauderdale that's not optional, it's table stakes.

> **Key Stat:** Broward County had **68.22 eviction filings per 1,000 renter households** in 2023, per the [Florida Housing Data Clearinghouse](https://flhousingdata.shimberg.ufl.edu/eviction-foreclosure/results?nid=600) — one of the densest distress concentrations in Florida.

Speed-to-lead on callbacks matters too. If someone calls back from a missed dial and you don't pick up within a few minutes, they're gone. Build a workflow around that — whether you're handling calls in-house or outsourcing to a service like [Televista](/services.html) that runs fully-managed outbound campaigns for real estate wholesalers.

Here's what actually moves the needle in a competitive market like Fort Lauderdale:

- **Segment your list** by distress type before calling
- **Use local South Florida caller IDs** on every dial session
- **Build separate scripts** for eviction, pre-foreclosure, and absentee
- **Track contact rate vs. appointment rate** — not just call volume
- **Follow up at least 5-7 times** before pulling a lead from rotation

One more thing people skip — call timing. In Florida, late morning (10am–12pm) and early afternoon (4pm–6pm) consistently outperform the rest of the day. Plan your dial blocks around that, not around your own schedule.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Most wholesalers working the Fort Lauderdale market burn their lists before they ever get going. Not because the leads are bad — with [Broward County projecting roughly 16,666 eviction filings in 2025](https://flhousingdata.shimberg.ufl.edu/eviction-foreclosure/results?nid=600), the distressed seller pool is still very much there — but because the *approach* is off from day one.

**Mistake #1: Calling with no script structure.** I've seen teams pull solid pre-foreclosure lists from [BatchLeads](https://www.batchleads.io) or [PropStream](https://www.propstream.com) and then improvise their way through every call. Doesn't work. Motivated sellers in Broward County are skeptical — they've often heard from multiple wholesalers already. You need an opener that sounds human, not a pitch.

> **Pro tip:** Don't open with "I'm looking to buy your house." Ask an open question about the property first. Sounds obvious, but most callers skip it entirely.

**Mistake #2: No follow-up system.** A huge percentage of deals don't close on the first call — they close on the third, fourth, or fifth touch. If you're not logging dispositions in [REsimpli](https://resimpli.com) or a proper CRM, you're losing those conversations permanently.

**Mistake #3: Hiring a generic outbound vendor.** Cold calling agencies that don't specialize in real estate cold calling Florida-style markets — high competition, motivated sellers, investor-to-seller conversations — tend to underperform badly. The script language, objection handling, and follow-up cadence for a distressed homeowner is completely different from a B2B sales call.

**Mistake #4: Ignoring compliance.** Florida has active DNC enforcement. Skipping scrubbing isn't just sloppy — it's a liability.

One more thing that's genuinely underrated: **not tracking connect rates by zip code.** Broward's neighborhoods vary wildly in pick-up behavior. Dial data from [Mojo Dialer](https://mojosells.com) can show you exactly where your connects are coming from — most people never look.
