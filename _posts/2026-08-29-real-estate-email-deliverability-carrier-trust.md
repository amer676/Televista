## What is Decoding Carrier Trust: Benchmarks for Optimal Outbound Deliverability in Real Estate?

**Carrier trust** — or sender reputation — is how email providers like Gmail, Outlook, and Yahoo decide whether your message deserves a spot in the inbox or gets buried in spam. Full stop.

Every email you send gets evaluated. Not by a human, by an algorithm. And that algorithm is tracking a handful of signals constantly: your **IP reputation**, your authentication setup (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), your bounce rate, how often recipients mark you as spam, and whether your sending patterns look like a legitimate business or a bulk blaster who bought a list off the internet.

Real estate email deliverability carrier trust scores are just the aggregate of all those signals. Think of it like a credit score — except for your email domain.

[NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/spam-email) has been clear that spam "clutters inboxes, slows down web servers, and costs time and money to manage." Carriers know this too, which is why they've built increasingly aggressive filters. You don't have to be sending garbage content to get flagged — a messy list, a missing DKIM record, or a sudden volume spike can do it on their own.

> **Pro tip:** Your domain reputation and your IP reputation are separate things. You can have a clean IP on a shared server and still tank your sender score because your domain has a history of high unsubscribe rates. Most agents I've talked to don't realize that distinction until something breaks.

[Realtor.com® PRO published a piece in April 2025](https://www.realtor.com/marketing/resources/9-email-marketing-metrics-every-real-estate-agent-should-track-and-how-to-measure-success) covering the nine email metrics every real estate agent should track — and deliverability-adjacent signals like open rates and bounce rates were front and center.

The benchmark piece of this isn't just "don't get flagged." It's knowing what *good* looks like across each signal so you're not flying blind. That's what we're actually decoding here.

## Key Strategies and Best Practices

Authentication first. Everything else is secondary.

If your domain doesn't have **SPF, DKIM, and DMARC** configured correctly, you're essentially sending emails without a return address — and carriers treat them accordingly. SPF tells receiving servers which IPs are allowed to send on your behalf. DKIM adds a cryptographic signature to each message. DMARC ties both together and tells servers what to do when something fails. All three. Not one, not two.

> **Pro tip:** Pull up [MXToolbox](https://mxtoolbox.com) right now and run your domain through their free lookup. If any of those three records are missing or misconfigured, fix that before you touch anything else in your email strategy. Seriously, everything else is noise until authentication is solid.

Once your authentication's clean, your list hygiene needs the same attention. Dead addresses, old contacts, people who haven't opened anything in 18+ months — they're dragging your sender reputation down every time you blast to them. [NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/spam-email) noted that spam clogs infrastructure and costs time and money, and that's exactly what a bloated, unvalidated list does to your own deliverability. Run your list through a validation tool like [NeverBounce](https://neverbounce.com) or [ZeroBounce](https://www.zerobounce.net) quarterly, minimum.

Sending volume [matters more than](https://televistaleadgeneration.com/blog/2024/03/18/weekly-reporting-matters-more-than-daily-call-volume/) most agents realize. Warming a new IP or domain by ramping up volume gradually — rather than blasting 5,000 contacts on day one — tells carriers you're a legitimate sender. Start small. 50 emails a day for the first week, double it the next, keep going until you hit your target volume. I've seen agents skip this entirely and wonder why their open rates tank immediately.

[Realtor.com® PRO published a breakdown](https://www.realtor.com/marketing/resources/9-email-marketing-metrics-every-real-estate-agent-should-track-and-how-to-measure-success) in April 2025 covering the nine metrics every agent should track — and the ones that directly feed your carrier trust score include **bounce rate, spam complaint rate, and unsubscribe rate**. Watch all three, not just opens.

Here's a quick reference for where your numbers should be:

| Metric | Acceptable Range | Red Flag Zone |
|---|---|---|
| Bounce Rate | Under 2% | Above 5% |
| Spam Complaint Rate | Under 0.1% | Above 0.3% |
| Unsubscribe Rate | Under 0.5% | Above 1% |
| Open Rate (Real Estate) | 20–30% | Under 15% |

Segmentation is underrated — honestly, most people skip it and pay for it later. Don't send the same email to active buyers, cold leads, and past clients. Carriers notice when engagement drops because you're sending irrelevant content to uninterested recipients.

And one thing almost nobody talks about: **your sending domain and your business domain shouldn't be the same** for high-volume campaigns. Use a subdomain (like `mail.yourdomain.com`) so if something goes sideways, your primary domain's reputation stays intact.

Google Postmaster Tools is criminally underused. Free, pulls data straight from Gmail, shows you exactly how your domain reputation trends over time — and most agents have never even heard of it.

> **Pro tip:** Set up Google Postmaster Tools before you do anything else. You can't fix a reputation problem you can't see.

## Step-by-Step Implementation

Authentication before anything else. Seriously — don't touch your list size, your send volume, or your subject lines until SPF, DKIM, and DMARC are confirmed working on your domain.

**Step 1: Authenticate your domain.** Log into your DNS provider (GoDaddy, Namecheap, Cloudflare — wherever your domain lives) and add your SPF record, DKIM key, and DMARC policy. Your ESP will give you the exact values. [MXToolbox](https://mxtoolbox.com) has a free lookup tool that'll show you exactly what's published and what's broken. Takes maybe 20 minutes if you've never done it.

**Step 2: Warm up your IP or domain.** If you're on a new sending domain or just switched ESPs, don't blast your whole list on day one. Start with your most engaged contacts — people who've opened in the last 30 days. Ramp volume slowly over 2-4 weeks. Carriers are watching that early engagement window closely.

**Step 3: Clean your list before every campaign.** Run your list through a verification tool ([NeverBounce](https://neverbounce.com) or [ZeroBounce](https://www.zerobounce.net) are both solid) before sending. A hard bounce rate above **2%** is a red flag carriers notice fast — [Realtor.com® PRO](https://www.realtor.com/marketing/resources/9-email-marketing-metrics-every-real-estate-agent-should-track-and-how-to-measure-success) tracks bounce rate as one of the nine metrics every agent should monitor. Don't skip this step.

> **Pro tip:** Set your DMARC policy to `p=quarantine` first, not `p=reject`. It's a softer landing while you're figuring out if any legitimate senders are failing auth — rejecting too early can kill your own emails.

**Step 4: Monitor reputation weekly.** [Google Postmaster Tools](https://postmaster.google.com) is free and shows your domain reputation and spam rate directly from Gmail's perspective. Check it. [NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/spam-email) has been clear that abusive email practices damage everyone in the space — your reputation isn't just your problem, it affects how carriers treat real estate senders broadly.

**Step 5: Suppress unengaged contacts.** Anyone who hasn't opened in 90+ days gets moved to a re-engagement sequence, not your main list. If they don't respond to that — remove them.

Four of these five steps cost nothing but time. The tool costs are minimal. There's no excuse for skipping them.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Most agents don't tank their sender reputation on purpose. It happens through neglect — small decisions that compound over months until carriers quietly start routing everything to spam.

**Buying or renting email lists is probably the fastest way to destroy your IP reputation.** Those lists are full of spam traps. One hit on a spam trap, and you're flagged. Carriers don't give you a warning first.

Skipping list hygiene is a close second. If you're not scrubbing bounced addresses out of your sequences regularly, your bounce rate climbs — and [NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/spam-email) has been clear since at least 2017 that fraudulent and abusive emailing practices hurt everyone in the industry, not just the sender. Your reputation drags down everyone on a shared IP.

> **Pro tip:** Set a calendar reminder every 60 days to pull your bounce report from whatever ESP you're using — [Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com), [ActiveCampaign](https://www.activecampaign.com), doesn't matter — and purge anything that hard-bounced. Takes 20 minutes. Saves your domain.

A few other mistakes I see constantly:

- **Blasting cold audiences with high-volume sends immediately** — carriers flag sudden volume spikes. Warm up first.
- **Ignoring unsubscribe requests** (even by one day) — this is a compliance issue, not just a reputation one.
- **Missing DMARC entirely** — SPF and DKIM without DMARC is half the job done.
- **Using a free domain** (Gmail, Yahoo) for bulk outreach — you're borrowing someone else's reputation and you'll lose every time.

[Realtor.com® PRO](https://www.realtor.com/marketing/resources/9-email-marketing-metrics-every-real-estate-agent-should-track-and-how-to-measure-success) published a full breakdown of the metrics agents should actually be watching — bounce rate, spam complaints, open rates — back in April 2025. Worth reading if you're not tracking all nine.

The mistake nobody talks about? Sending the same email to your entire list regardless of engagement. Carriers notice when 60% of recipients never open anything. Segment. Send to the engaged contacts first.
