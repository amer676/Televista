## How to Choose a Cold Calling Company — 6 Things to Actually Check

You've compared the options. Now you're about to sign something. Before you do, run through these six checks — because the wrong call center doesn't just waste money, it can expose you to legal liability that turns a $2,000/month vendor into a six-figure problem.

**1. Confirm they work real estate specifically — not just "sales."**

Generic B2B callers don't know the difference between a motivated seller and someone who just refinanced. The objections are different. The qualifying questions are different. Ask what asset classes they've called and what the disqualifying criteria are for a lead. Vague answers here are a red flag.

**2. Ask exactly where their lists come from and how often they're scrubbed.**

Do they pull from [BatchLeads](https://batchleads.io) or [PropStream](https://propstream.com)? Do they build their own? Either can work — but per the [Televista compliance blog](https://televistaleadgeneration.com/blog/2026/03/18/2026-real-estate-cold-calling-compliance-guide/), call lists must be scrubbed against the National Do Not Call Registry **every 31 days**. If they can't tell you their scrub frequency off the top of their head, that's a problem.

**3. Ask for a sample call recording before you commit. Non-negotiable.**

Not a highlight reel — an actual recent call, objections and all. You'll learn more from three minutes of audio than from any sales deck they send over.

**4. Understand their reporting cadence.**

Daily dashboards vs. weekly PDF summaries are genuinely different operating philosophies. Teams running tools like [CallTools](https://calltools.com) tend to have real-time visibility. If a vendor can't tell you what happened yesterday, you won't know what's working.

**5. Pin down their compliance posture explicitly.**

TCPA violations run **$500–$1,500 per call**, and the [FCC's 2024 one-to-one consent rule](https://televistaleadgeneration.com/blog/2026/03/18/2026-real-estate-cold-calling-compliance-guide/) changed the game entirely. Ask how they document consent. Ask what happens if a DNC number slips through. A single non-compliant campaign can mean five- or six-figure exposure in damages — more on this in the next section.

**6. Get their definition of "appointment" in writing.**

"They seemed interested" isn't an appointment. A confirmed two-way calendar hold with a contact name, phone number, and agreed time is. Most disappointments with calling vendors come down to this one unresolved ambiguity — I've seen it more than once, honestly.

> **Pro tip:** Ask any vendor you're evaluating how they handle no-shows and reschedules. The answer tells you whether they're tracking quality or just activity.

## TCPA, DNC, and the 2024 FCC Rule Change — What Fort Lauderdale Investors Need to Know

Cold calling is still legal in 2026. [Completely legal](https://televistaleadgeneration.com/blog/2026/03/18/2026-real-estate-cold-calling-compliance-guide/), still effective, still one of the best ways to find off-market deals in a market as competitive as Fort Lauderdale. But the rules got a lot more serious in 2024, and most investors have no idea.

The [FCC's one-to-one consent rule](https://televistaleadgeneration.com/blog/2026/03/18/2026-real-estate-cold-calling-compliance-guide/) changed how outbound calling works at a fundamental level. Every phone number you dial now requires **explicit, written consent** — tied to your specific company, not just a blanket opt-in buried in some lead form. You can't buy a list from [BatchLeads](https://batchleads.io) or [PropStream](https://propstream.com) and just start dialing anymore. Not without documentation proving that contact actually agreed to hear from *you*.

Operationally, that means your workflow needs to account for three things:

- **Consent documentation** — written records showing when and how each contact opted in
- **DNC scrubbing** — [call lists must be scrubbed against the National Do Not Call Registry every 31 days](https://televistaleadgeneration.com/blog/2026/03/18/2026-real-estate-cold-calling-compliance-guide/), no exceptions
- **Opt-out handling** — if someone says don't call again, that request has to be honored immediately and logged

Miss any of those? [TCPA violations run $500 to $1,500 per call or text](https://televistaleadgeneration.com/blog/2026/03/18/2026-real-estate-cold-calling-compliance-guide/). Not per campaign. Per call. A single non-compliant campaign can snowball into [five- or six-figure exposure](https://televistaleadgeneration.com/blog/2026/03/18/2026-real-estate-cold-calling-compliance-guide/) fast — and the [FTC has been ramping up enforcement](https://televistaleadgeneration.com/blog/2026/03/18/2026-real-estate-cold-calling-compliance-guide/) across telemarketing broadly.

Most people skip vetting their vendor on this. Don't.

Before you sign with any real estate cold calling service, ask these questions directly:

- How do you document consent for each contact on the list?
- What's your DNC scrub frequency and which registry do you pull from?
- How do you handle opt-outs mid-campaign?
- Can you show your compliance process in writing?

If they fumble any of those — or worse, look confused — walk away. You're the one liable, not them.

> **Pro tip:** A compliant vendor won't hesitate to answer these questions. They'll have a documented process ready. If they're vague or say "we handle it, don't worry" — that's your answer right there.

**Snowbirds are real — and so is the window.** Late fall through early spring, you've got a wave of part-time residents making housing decisions. That's when motivation spikes on both ends. Fort Lauderdale's waterfront corridors are packed with units where acquisition strategy looks nothing like a standard SFR market. Seller motivations differ. HOA complications affect exit options. Callers who don't understand that will blow the conversation fast.

> **Pro tip:** Pull separate condo-specific lists and use different scripts — the qualifying questions aren't the same as a single-family absentee owner call.
